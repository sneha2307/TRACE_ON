About TraceOn

TraceOn is a criminal network analysis system built to help investigators connect the dots between FIRs, people, phones, accounts, vehicles and biometric evidence.

Why TraceOn

Police stations register a large number of FIRs every year, but the same phone number, vehicle, account or fingerprint can quietly reappear across cases filed months apart and in different stations. Investigated on their own, these cases look unrelated. TraceOn brings the records together and surfaces those shared links automatically.

What it does

Given a FIR number, TraceOn searches past records for people, phones, accounts, vehicles and biometrics that match, then builds a network showing how everyone involved is connected \u2013 directly or through a chain of shared evidence. Matches are ranked into leads, mapped visually, and can be explored with a built-in case assistant.

Crime dashboard

A state, district and police-station level dashboard shows crime trends and hotspots, helping identify where a particular offence is concentrated and rising, in the same spirit as national crime-tracking systems used by police departments today.

A note on the data

This is a prototype. Sample FIRs and matches are used to demonstrate the workflow; every lead it surfaces is meant to be reviewed and confirmed by an investigator, not treated as a finding on its own.

Try one of these

FIR records

CSV columns

fir_no,date,state,district,station,crime_type,sections,status,io_name,io_rank,io_id,io_phone,brief,person,role,role_note,type,value

One row per fact. A person row records who was in the FIR (roles: accused, suspect, victim, witness). Add the same person again with a type and value for each connection: phone, account, vehicle, address, location, associate, fingerprint or retina. A row with no person attaches the item to the whole FIR. A fingerprint or retina with no person means it was found at the scene.

Sample data is made up. Matches are leads for an investigator to review, not findings of guilt.
